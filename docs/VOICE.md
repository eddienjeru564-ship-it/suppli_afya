# Voice and copy rules

The test: it should sound like someone who understands a distributor's business explaining
Suppli Afya clearly and confidently to another distributor. Not software marketing.

## Always

- Describe the distributor's real day: WhatsApp chats, status updates, M-Pesa messages, chama
  meetings, customers who say "I'll pay on Friday".
- Explain features by where they fit in the workflow and why they matter. Features support the
  story; they are not the story.
- Use complete sentences. Vary their length. Let some sections breathe as prose.
- Let some headlines simply say what the thing does ("Each morning, a short list of who to talk
  to, and why"). Not every headline needs to be a promise.
- Keep it commercially confident: orders, payments, reorders, getting paid.
- Be specific and local where it helps (sukuma, chai with sugar, Till or Paybill), never as decoration.

## Never

- "AI", "automation", "powered by", "smart", "revolutionary", "seamless", "unlock", "supercharge",
  "digital transformation", "all-in-one", "game-changer".
- Stacked fragments. Punchy one-word sentences. Rows of full stops for effect.
- Artificial contrasts ("It's not X. It's Y.") unless the contrast is the actual point.
- Fake numbers, fake testimonials, fake logos, "trusted by thousands". Use real ones or none.
- Health claims. Nothing on the site or in the check says a product treats, cures or prevents
  a disease. The distributor's reputation depends on this.
- Anything that suggests we are BF Suma or endorsed by BF Suma.

## Before publishing a change

Read the whole page top to bottom as one piece. Remove anything that repeats an earlier section,
sounds polished for its own sake, or could appear on any other company's website.

## Customer-facing voice (the health check)

Calm, warm and careful. Short questions, one idea per screen, a helper line only where it helps.
"Why we ask" lines explain sensitive questions. Results start with what we heard, then the plan,
then what we left out and why, then habits, then the handoff. No selling language in the check:
the distributor does the selling.
